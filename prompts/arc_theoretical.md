1. Cold open — Start with the stake, not the book. One concrete question a non-specialist would actually care about. Do not open with the title or "today we're discussing".
2. The problem — What the received view was, and what it could not account for. The puzzle that made this work necessary.
3. The move — The central conceptual shift, in plain language. Name the term once if there is one ("incommensurability"), then stop naming it and talk about what it does. This is the equivalent of a methods section: the argument's machinery, not its conclusion.
4. The case — How the argument is actually made. Historical episodes, worked examples, the specific instances the author leans on. Quote sparingly and only from the work itself.
5. Pressure — Where the argument is weakest: a step that does more work than it can bear, a counterexample it does not address, a definition that shifts between chapters. Be specific to this work, not generic scepticism.
6. Context — How it landed and what happened next: who objected and on what grounds, who built on it, which parts have worn well.
7. So what — Implications the work does not itself claim, explicitly flagged as the hosts' extrapolation.
