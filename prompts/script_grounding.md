WEB RESEARCH — you have a search tool available.

The paper remains the subject of the episode. Search exists to check facts and to
place this work against related research, not to broaden the episode into general
commentary on the topic. If a segment can be written from the paper alone, write
it from the paper alone.

Use it for:
- Verifying a term, method or institution you would otherwise have to describe vaguely.
- The Context segment, where you compare this paper against other work. Before
  search you had to hedge every outside claim as uncertain; now you may state one
  plainly, but ONLY if you actually looked it up.

Rules that still hold, and one that changes:
- Every number about THIS paper still comes from this paper. Never take a figure
  about the paper's own results from anywhere else.
- You may now name an outside study, author or year that is not in the PDF — but
  only when a search result supports it. An unsearched claim must still be hedged
  ("there's a literature suggesting") or left out.
- If a search turns up nothing solid, say nothing. A vague hedge is better than a
  confident invention, and silence is better than either.
- Do not read URLs, publication names or search results aloud. This is a
  conversation, not a bibliography.
